2.1 From sound to prediction

A model never hears a whole recording at once. It listens to a fixed window of audio, a few seconds long, turns it into a spectrogram (a picture of sound energy across time and frequency) and returns a score for every species it knows. Sliding the window along the recording gives a score for each species in each window.

The evaluation then turns those window scores into claims about two things: which species are present in each labelled 5-second segment, and which species are present in each recording. The figure shows one excerpt. BirdNET and Perch slice the same audio differently: BirdNET into 3-second windows, Perch into 5-second windows.

2.2 Dataset: BirdSet

BirdSet (Rauch et al. 2025, ICLR 2025, arXiv:2403.10380) is a benchmark for bird-sound classification. Its training set, XCL, holds 528,434 xeno-canto recordings of 9,734 classes; its test data are eight soundscape datasets annotated by experts, more than 400 hours in all (published figures). Here every test site is scored: {{n_recordings_total}} recordings, {{hours_total|int}} hours.

  • POW is BirdSet's validation set, not a test set. It is included here like the others.
  • NBP is not a soundscape set. Its “recordings” are clips of a few seconds each, so its recording-level results mean little.
  • Most sites record only around dawn. SSW records around the clock and UHH from early morning to late evening, so only those two show full daily patterns. BirdSet gives PER's and SSW's times in UTC (their file names end in Z); they are converted to local time ({{file:scripts/site_calls.py}}). NBP has no clock times or frequency boxes.
  • Site biomes are WWF biomes assigned per site ({{file:configs/reference/sites.csv}}). The sites are small areas, so a label describes the site's setting, not every recorder.

Label check. Before relying on BirdSet's 5-second labels, they were rebuilt from its call-level annotations (a call counts for a segment when it overlaps it by at least 0.5 s) and compared with the official labels, from metadata alone ({{file:scripts/birdset_labels.py}}). They agree exactly on {{label_agree_lo|pct}} to {{label_agree_hi|pct}} of segments at {{label_agree_n_sites}} sites ({{label_agree_pow|pct}} at POW), and on {{label_agree_nbp|pct}} at NBP.

Two modes. Clips mode follows BirdSet's protocol: each site's 5-second test clips, every species in the site's label set. It is the mode that can be compared with published figures. Soundscape mode runs the models over the full recordings, as a deployment would, and scores each 5-second segment and each recording. Both are reported because they answer different questions: does the harness reproduce the literature, and how would the models do on the recordings a monitoring project collects.

Species information. To ask which kinds of species are served poorly, each species is described from outside sources: body mass, lifestyle and habitat from AVONET; diet, foraging stratum and a nocturnal flag from EltonTraits 1.0, matched to eBird names through AVONET's crosswalk; the number of xeno-canto recordings, queried on {{xc_query_date}}; and, from BirdSet's call annotations, each species' call frequency band and calling times at each site. The xeno-canto counts are today's counts, larger than what any model trained on, and xeno-canto's IOC names can split or lump species differently from eBird's. These sources are how “underrepresented” is measured in 3.3.

2.4 Running the models side by side

Comparing the models fairly took four decisions:

  • One package for all three. BirdNET 2.4, BirdNET 3.0 and Perch V2 all run through the birdnet package, with the same audio loading and the same window settings per model.
  • One label space. Every model's labels are mapped to scientific names in the 2025 eBird taxonomy, through eBird codes and {{file:configs/taxonomy/synonyms.yaml}} for names that changed. Every species at every site is scored for every model, including the {{uncovered_b24}} species BirdNET 2.4 cannot predict (at {{uncovered_b24_sites}}), which count against it.
  • Raw logits, cached before any scoring decision. Each model's raw output is stored per file, so every later choice (score scale, alignment, metric) is applied to the same predictions.
  • One alignment rule for matching model windows to labelled segments, set out below.
{{block:fig_pipeline}}

Design decision: the alignment rule

Model windows and BirdSet's 5-second segments lie on different grids, so each window has to be assigned to a segment. Under the first rule tried, any window overlapping a segment by at least 1 second counted for it. Perch's 5-second windows then scored calls in the neighbouring segment: on POW its soundscape cmAP was 0.471 against 0.567 on clips (historical numbers, under the old rule, from {{file:decision_point.md}}).

The rule now used is the half-window rule: a window counts for the segment that holds more than half of it. It follows from the window geometry alone and was not tuned on results. Perch on POW now scores {{perch_pow_clips_cmap}} on clips and {{perch_pow_scape_cmap}} in the soundscapes.

Three class scopes. Each result can be computed over all of a site's species (all), over the species a given model can predict (covered), or over the species every model can predict (shared). This page uses all, BirdSet's protocol, unless stated.

Three-state labels. A species is present, absent, or not reviewed in each segment. Audio that nobody reviewed is left out of every metric rather than counted as absent.

2.5 Evaluation criteria

Each metric answers a different question, and each matters here for a different reason.

cmAP (primary)
Average precision per species, averaged over species. Because each species counts once, rare and underrepresented species weigh as much as common ones, which is the point of this project.
AUROC
The chance that a segment with the species scores higher than one without it, averaged over species. It does not depend on how common a species is, so it compares fairly across sites and hours.
Top-1
The share of segments with at least one label where the top-scoring species is one of them. Reported for comparability with BirdSet.
Recording-level presence
The deployment question: is species X in this recording? Recording cmAP is cmAP over recordings instead of segments. Recall at 80, 90 and 95% precision is the share of true presences found at the single score threshold where that share of claims is right. List precision is the share of a recording's claimed species that are really there. In clips mode each clip is its own recording.
Evidence precision
A detection is only useful if a person can check it. For each species claimed at the 90%-precision threshold, the model cites its highest-scoring window as evidence; the evidence is right when that window overlaps an annotated call of the species by at least 0.5 s. The conditional version counts only claims that are right at recording level, separating “wrong species” from “right species, wrong clip”.

Uncertainty. Intervals are 95% bootstrap intervals that resample whole recordings, because segments within a recording are correlated: resampling segments would make the intervals too narrow.

Per-species reporting. A group mean can hide species that fail completely, so results are also given per species (3.3 and 3.4). Per-species AP is noisy for species with only a few labelled segments; group means pool many species, but their intervals still widen as groups get small.
