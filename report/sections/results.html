3.1 Replication

Before measuring anything new, the harness has to reproduce what has been published. Perch V2 in clips mode is compared with the three Perch 2.0 variants in van Merriënboer et al. 2025 (arXiv:2508.04665, Appendix C, Table 4). A value counts as within when it is no more than {{repl_tol|2f}} outside the published range. {{repl_n_within}} of {{repl_n_total}} values on {{repl_n_sites}} sites are within; none falls more than {{repl_max_below}} below the published range.

3.3 Where performance is weakest

“Underrepresented” has two meanings here, and they are kept apart. Training representation (how many xeno-canto recordings a species has; which region and biome it lives in) is what the project aims to change. Test prevalence (how many labelled segments a species has at a site) mostly limits how precisely its performance can be measured.

  • Biome. Tropical moist broadleaf forest ({{biome_tropical_sites}}; {{biome_tropical_pairs}} site–species pairs) has mean per-species AP of {{biome_tropical_range}} across models, the lowest biome for {{biome_tropical_lowest_n}} of {{n_models}} models. Temperate conifer forest ({{biome_temperate_conifer_sites}}) is at {{biome_temperate_conifer_range}}, temperate broadleaf and mixed forest ({{biome_temperate_broadleaf_sites}}) at {{biome_temperate_broadleaf_range}}, and the Mediterranean site ({{biome_mediterranean_sites}}; {{biome_mediterranean_pairs}} pairs) at {{biome_mediterranean_range}}. With one to three sites per biome, a biome's result is largely its sites' results.
  • Xeno-canto recordings. Species with under 100 xeno-canto recordings ({{xc_under100_pairs}} pairs) average {{xc_under100_range}} AP across models, against {{xc_2000plus_range}} for species with 2,000 or more ({{xc_2000plus_pairs}} pairs). The gap is smallest for BirdNET 2.4 ({{xc_under100_b24}} against {{xc_2000plus_b24}}) and largest for BirdNET 3.0 ({{xc_under100_b30}} against {{xc_2000plus_b30}}). {{xc_nodata_pairs}} pairs have no xeno-canto count yet.
  • Labelled examples. Species with 1–9 labelled segments average {{prev_1_9_range}} AP across models, 10–49 segments {{prev_10_49_range}}, and 50 or more {{prev_50plus_range}}. Part of this gap is measurement: AP from a handful of positives is noisy.

Each of these claims rests on groups of at least ten site–species pairs. The explorer's Groups view shows these and other groupings (habitat, body mass, diet, call frequency) with intervals, for every site or one at a time.

The weakest species. A fixed rule, set before looking at the result: in clips mode, among the (site, species) pairs with at least {{weakest_min_pos}} labelled segments, the {{weakest_rule_n}} with the lowest best AP over the three models. A low value means every model struggles with that species at that site. {{weakest_top_site_n}} of the {{weakest_n}} are at {{weakest_top_site}}, and no model reaches AP {{weakest_max_ap|2f}} on any of them.

3.4 Species-level data and the explorer

The explorer holds every result above at finer grain. Choose clips or soundscapes and one site or all of them, then a view:

  • Overview: each model at each site, with the best value per site in bold.
  • Groups: mean per-species AP by trait, biome, prevalence, call frequency or xeno-canto count.
  • Species: one row per site and species. Pick the metric, compare two models in the difference column, search by name, family or habitat, hide species with fewer than ten positives, and add trait columns. Click a name for its traits, calling times by local hour, call frequency band, and links to xeno-canto, eBird and the Macaulay Library. Calling activity is calls per recorded hour; hours a site never recorded are shown as unknown.
  • Hours: bird activity and model performance by local hour of day, from the soundscapes.
