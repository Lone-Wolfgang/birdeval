Purpose. This page is groundwork for a project to improve bird-sound recognition for the species and biomes that current models serve poorly. It is not a leaderboard. Its job is to describe where three widely used models stand today, measured carefully enough that a later improvement can be told apart from noise.

Improving on existing models starts with understanding them. The leading models share a broad approach: they turn audio into a spectrogram and score every species they know. They differ in many design decisions: how long a window they listen to, the sample rate, the training data, the label set and the scale of their output.

Running and comparing them fairly is harder than it looks. Species names have to be mapped onto one taxonomy, scores put on a common footing, and each model's windows matched to labels that were drawn on a different grid. Methods (section 2) explains how each of these is handled.

The page is organised around four aims:

  1. Replicate published results before proposing any improvement.
  2. Understand how the models work and where they differ mechanically.
  3. Define the evaluation criteria, and why each matters for passive soundscape monitoring, where a wrong detection costs more than a missed one.
  4. Establish a baseline by site, biome and species, and find where performance is weakest.

  • Weakest areas. Performance is weakest in tropical moist forest, where mean per-species AP is {{biome_tropical_range}} across models against {{biome_others_range}} in the other biomes. It is also weaker for species with few xeno-canto recordings and for species with few labelled examples (3.3).
  • Mechanics change conclusions. Perch's raw scores sit at different levels for different species, so a single deployment threshold serves it badly even though it ranks recordings well: its recording-level cmAP is {{scape_rec_cmap_perch}}, close to BirdNET's {{scape_rec_cmap_b24}} and {{scape_rec_cmap_b30}}, but its recall at 90% precision is {{scape_rec_recall90_perch}} against {{scape_rec_recall90_b24}} and {{scape_rec_recall90_b30}} (3.2).

How to read this page. Methods (section 2) covers the data, the models, the pipeline and the metrics. Results 3.4 holds the species tables: per-species metrics, traits, calling times, call frequencies and links to xeno-canto, eBird and the Macaulay Library for each species at each site.

BirdNET may have been trained on recordings from these test sites, so its results here may be optimistic (2.3).
